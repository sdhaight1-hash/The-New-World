## Historical and material themes

Ranching families, cattle and horses, hay, irrigation, grazing access, winter feed, water rights, animal husbandry, land tenure, county institutions and settlements. Existing tribal nations and reservation institutions possess their own political continuity, rights and local histories; treat each distinctly, rather than as generic counterparts to ranch landholders.

## Plague and Cascade hypotheses

Remote ranches may see fewer early pathogen introductions, but sparse healthcare and emergency distances can create severe consequences once illness arrives. Post-Cascade ranches may retain herds, workshops, equipment and practical expertise, yet still need veterinary supplies, feed production, transport and cooperative institutions.

## Year 50 questions

Which towns and ranches survive? What does regional food balance permit? How do existing land/property/tribal rights evolve? Which areas retain seasonal markets, water access and viable trade? Does hereditary ranch influence emerge, or do federated community institutions remain stronger?
