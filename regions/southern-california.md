# Southern California — Big Bear and Victor Valley

**Status:** one initial detailed focus; open boundaries. Reference era Year ~50 after Cascade. Connected areas (LA Basin, Inland Empire, San Bernardino foothills, Cajon Pass, Lucerne Valley and northbound routes) are part of the causal history, not excluded.

## Real geographical baseline

- Big Bear Lake/Big Bear City/Fawnskin/Baldwin Lake: mountain valley, lake, forests, cooler summers, snowy winters, short growing seasons, constrained agriculture and groundwater/spring-related drinking-water systems. The lake should not be casually treated as the municipal water supply.
- Victorville/Hesperia/Apple Valley/Adelanto and smaller settlements: High Desert, Mojave River corridor, groundwater basins, extensive suburban/industrial salvage and roads. The Mojave River is often dry at the surface: map dependable wells and pumping options, not imaginary perennial streams.
- The San Bernardino Mountains make the relationship to the LA basin different from the highly interconnected urban LA–western IE continuum. Cajon Pass is important, **not the only access**.

## Alternate history: working outcomes

1. **Plague:** Los Angeles and much of the western Inland Empire suffer exceptional direct mortality amid early, widespread metropolitan transmission. Big Bear and Victor Valley fare comparatively better owing to later introductions, restricted access, locally successful containment and chance. The Cajon Quarantine is a promising event; dates, actors, legal authority and exact boundaries remain open.
2. **Interlude:** In the years between plague and Cascade, the less-affected mountain/desert communities retain more population and skills than much of the southern metro system. Some refugees arrive before the solar events.
3. **Cascade:** A struggling but recovering region loses dependable industrial networks and food/fuel deliveries. Water pumping is a critical problem in Victor Valley; Big Bear faces winter, food capacity and transport constraints.
4. **Migration:** Many Southern Californians travel toward Big Bear or northward into agricultural California; some continue toward northern Sacramento Valley. Not all reach northern California, and not all desert residents leave.
5. **Year 50:** LA basin's former metropolitan dominance has collapsed; Big Bear and Victor Valley together are a significant surviving Southern California hub. Victor Valley retains **smaller holdout communities** rather than becoming an empty ruin. Their mutual trade and political relationship is open.

## Candidate community studies (not assigned fates)

Big Bear Lake, Big Bear City, Fawnskin, Baldwin Lake, Victorville, Hesperia, Apple Valley, Oro Grande, Helendale, Lucerne Valley, Phelan, Wrightwood, and routes toward the San Bernardino foothills.

## Key constraints before inventing population or borders

- Verify well depths, groundwater basins, pump alternatives and regional water balance.
- Model agricultural land, crop yields, livestock feed and food imports rather than assuming mountain forests feed a large population.
- Separate **pandemic death**, **post-Cascade mortality**, **out-migration** and **later births**.
- Document pass roads, freight infrastructure, seasonal snow and summer heat.
- Keep refuge, land control, councils, dynasties and rivalries **provisional** until resource and population models are built.
