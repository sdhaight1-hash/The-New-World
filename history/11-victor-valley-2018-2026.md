# Victor Valley and the Mojave River corridor — documented local baseline, 2018 to 17 November 2026

**Broader scope:** [Southern California starting-region history](07-southern-california-local-2018-2026.md) ← [California](04-california-2018-2026.md). The focus includes Victorville, Hesperia, Apple Valley, Adelanto, Oro Grande, Helendale and associated High Desert infrastructure. Distinct nearby communities are not collapsed into a single municipality.

## Geography and settlement patterns

The Victor Valley lies north of the San Bernardino Mountains and links to the Inland Empire through Cajon Pass, Interstate 15 and additional routes. A key feature is the **Mojave River and associated groundwater basins**: water may flow underground even where the channel is dry on the surface. Wells, aquifer recharge, adjudicated pumping rights, imported supplies and treatment plants are separate parts of the water system. An assumption of a perennial surface river would be a serious geographic error.

The valley's cities and towns developed through housing expansion, commuting, defense-related and industrial land uses, warehousing, retail, healthcare, schools, local government and construction. Settlements spread across broad low-density tracts, creating substantial transport and infrastructure distances.

## 2018–2019: Expanding logistics and commuting

Southern California Association of Governments' city profiles describe Victorville and the wider region as part of a metropolitan trading system connected to Los Angeles and the Inland Empire. Warehousing and fulfillment centers near I-15 and rail routes are significant; many households depend on work or trade connected to the basin on the other side of the mountains.

The San Bernardino County Transportation Authority's 2024 draft Victor Valley subarea plan records **122,934 households in 2019** for its defined subarea of Adelanto, Apple Valley, Hesperia and Victorville. The planning area's geography must be retained with any population estimate; it is not necessarily identical to a broader cultural definition of 'Victor Valley.' Forecasts for 2035 and 2050 in that plan are **real-world planning projections, not established population after The New World's fictional disasters**.

## 2020–2022: COVID, real-estate changes and essential services

Pandemic restrictions and economic changes affect schools, healthcare, retail, restaurants and travel, but warehouses, logistics and some service operations continue. Remote work and housing affordability can alter relocation decisions. Victor Valley residents' exposure to distant employers may differ from exposure within the town of residence.

The 2024 Victor Valley Transit Authority planning report identifies population growth among key communities between 2010 and 2022, with differing rates by jurisdiction. Do not infer that any particular migration flow was caused solely by COVID-19 or that all growth continued uniformly to 2026.

## Water institutions and physical dependence

The **Mojave Basin Area adjudication** arises from a 1990 lawsuit and culminates in a 1996 judgment. Mojave Water Agency's Watermaster records production, rights transfers and administrative reports across many wells and producing parties. Legal production allowances do not imply physically available water during an electrical failure; wells may depend on grid power, generators, particular pump designs, maintenance and spare parts.

The 2026 Watermaster record includes debates and filings about production safe yield and free production allowances. Historical rights should be mapped by basin and provider, not treated as simple sovereign ownership after a fictional state collapse. A community's realistic water access depends on well location, lift, aquifer conditions, stored water and operations.

## 2023–2024: Regional planning and weather

The 2023 California atmospheric rivers and mountain storms affect roads, runoff and some desert channels differently from high-altitude snow areas. A flash flood in a wash is not the same as dependable year-round flow. Victor Valley transport planners address highway capacity, transit and future housing/employment patterns; these are evidence of contemporary priorities, not proof that forecast projects will be completed after the fictional divergence.

The 2024 draft transportation plan cites projected household and job growth through 2050. Those projections assume an ordinary development trajectory and cannot be transplanted into a setting where AGI disruption, a fictional epidemic and a solar catastrophe intervene.

## 2025–17 November 2026: Labor and trade dependencies

Regional logistics, food retail, schools, healthcare, government, construction, utilities and commuting remain economically relevant. San Bernardino County's broader trade ties and AI-related warehouse optimization may affect local occupations, but *documented* 2026 unemployment should not be replaced with a fictional 2028 mass-automation statistic.

A 2026 planning document for Hesperia cites a regional population estimate of **586,000 in 2025** for a particular planning region. This is a source-specific estimate using that region's boundary, not an undisputed census count or a valid estimate for the future post-Plague population. Carefully record the boundary and estimation method before comparing it with historical census tables.

## Settlement differentiation for later research

**Victorville:** road/rail service, warehouses, schools, local medical institutions, industrial land and Mojave River basin infrastructure.

**Hesperia:** wide residential geography, I-15 and local road links, housing growth and water-supply operations; some households' employment and supplies depend on regional mobility.

**Apple Valley:** town institutions, residential areas, groundwater providers, schools and regional services.

**Adelanto:** industrial sites, transport linkages, local water and government services; specific development projects must be confirmed individually.

**Oro Grande and Helendale:** smaller corridor settlements with different service distances and dependence on Victorville/nearby centers; absence from a city-level dataset is not evidence that they ceased to exist.

**Cajon Pass:** road and rail chokepoint but **not the only possible route** between mountains and deserts. A proposed fictional quarantine needs a full road graph, legal authority, personnel and alternate-access analysis before it can be assigned a date.

## Inventory needed before fictional settlement histories

Collect a dated census/estimate per community, water-system and well map, hospital and ambulance travel times, fuel and power distribution, food warehouses, rail spurs, freight depots, manufacturing/repair shops, farming and grazing land, roads by seasonal reliability, and local legal jurisdictions. The ability to salvage a warehouse full of parts is different from the ability to operate a modern distribution network.

The prior worldbuilding direction that **Victor Valley contains surviving holdout communities after the later Plague and Cascade** is a fictional outcome, not a demographic fact in November 2026. This chapter establishes the baseline from which that outcome can be credibly modeled.
