# Wyoming ranchlands and reservation-area starting study — 2018 to 17 November 2026

**Parent:** [Wyoming](06-wyoming-2018-2026.md). **Focus:** ranchlands between a place called 'Diamond Ranch' and an unspecified reservation. **Geographic identity deliberately unresolved.** **Separate speculative study:** [Wyoming after the Great Plague](../regions/wyoming.md).

## A necessary geographical limit

No defensible local historical chronology can be assigned to a specific Wyoming county, ranch, river or tribal nation until the intended Diamond Ranch and reservation are identified. Wyoming has multiple possible ranch name matches and distinct tribal jurisdictions. Guessing would introduce false facts into the world bible. The correct approach is to document **known state and material conditions**, then list the local historical records needed after identification.

Importantly, the Wind River Indian Reservation does exist in Wyoming, but its existence does not establish that it is the reservation intended in this scenario. Distinguish a factual description of Wyoming's tribal geography from the still-unresolved location reference.

## 2018–2019: Ranching and land tenure

The relevant local economy is expected to involve some combination of cattle production, grazing, hay meadows, irrigation, horses, fences, livestock transport, ranch workshops and trade with nearby towns. However, **which watershed, irrigated acreage, county road or town** supplies those functions remains unknown.

Real ranches require secure seasonal water, legal grazing access, winter feed, stock-health services and market transport. Land may be privately owned, federally administered or tribally held under different legal regimes. Surface-water rights, groundwater rights and grazing leases cannot be inferred from a property's name or the identity of its occupants.

## 2020–2021: Pandemic under rural constraints

In real Wyoming, COVID-19 disrupts healthcare and working life. Ranch activities and emergency services continue amid changing public-health measures, prices and access to care. A remote ranch may have fewer casual social contacts than a city workplace but must interact with farm suppliers, auction markets, veterinarians, fuel dealers, schools and medical providers.

When modeling a distinct fictional plague around 2030, don't assume that 2020 healthcare access or pathogen exposure exactly predicts later outcomes. Map the nearest hospital and emergency vehicle travel time only after the county is known.

## 2022–2023: Fuel prices, weather and grazing

Energy markets and national inflation affect diesel, feed, irrigation pumps, fencing materials and freight. Drought or winter storms can force supplemental feeding or affect livestock markets. The exact incidence of 2018–2026 weather events cannot be assigned to an unidentified ranch; data from the right weather station and watershed will be essential once the geography is identified.

Hay production is an annual and seasonal flow, not an indefinite store. Herd size, pregnancy/lambing/calving, age structure, animal disease, equipment and worker capacity must be considered before extrapolating a local post-Cascade food economy.

## 2024–17 November 2026: Energy, markets and local institutions

Wyoming's statewide energy output is large relative to population, but a ranch's access to electricity depends on local distribution, backup generators, fuel and maintenance. Livestock markets rely on processing capacity, inspections and long-distance transportation. Communities contain legal and civic institutions beyond private landholders: schools, sheriffs, county government, local associations, tribal governments where applicable, and local healthcare.

**Tribal sovereignty:** if the focus borders reservation land, the applicable tribal nation and authorities must be identified before proposing treaties, police jurisdictions, trade agreements or later successor states. Tribal institutions are not generic ranch councils, and their rights cannot be assumed to vanish during a crisis.

## Local historical dossier template — blocked on identification

1. **Place:** canonical name, precise county, nearby towns, highway access, water basin, altitude, winter conditions and federal/state/tribal/private land ownership.
2. **Ranch:** land boundaries, herd, forage acreage, water rights, hay yields, equipment, workshops, fuel and households as of a specified year.
3. **Reservation:** tribal nation, government, cultural and legal institutions, agencies, schools, health services, population and recognized jurisdiction.
4. **Trade:** livestock markets, feed and diesel suppliers, processors, machinery service, nearby rail and hospital.
5. **2018–2026 events:** weather, fires, elections, laws, local economic changes and documented community events from county-specific archives.
6. **Future divergence:** model 2027–2029 automation effects on ranch accounting, distribution, energy and supply chains before assessing the Great Plague.

**No county-specific event, treaty, named family, tribal leader, or post-Cascade institution has been invented.** This dossier is intentionally less detailed than the Oregon and California local studies until its geography is settled.
