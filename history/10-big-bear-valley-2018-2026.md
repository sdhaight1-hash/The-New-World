# Big Bear Valley — documented local baseline, 2018 to 17 November 2026

**Broader scope:** [Southern California starting-region history](07-southern-california-local-2018-2026.md) ← [California](04-california-2018-2026.md). This chapter deliberately concentrates on mountain settlements, winter access, groundwater, seasonal visitors, fire and emergency services. The 2030 fictional Great Plague is outside this historical period.

## Settlements and landscape

The focus includes Big Bear Lake, Big Bear City, Fawnskin, Baldwin Lake and neighboring mountain communities at high elevation in the San Bernardino Mountains. There are substantial differences among incorporated city government, unincorporated communities, water providers and surrounding federal forest land. A valley-scale historical description should not assign all residents to one municipality or water district.

Tourism, ski and lake recreation, vacation-property ownership, construction, education, retail, healthcare, restaurants, lodging and maintenance are part of the labor system. **Seasonal population and permanent population are different measurements.** The DWP reports an average annual population served of roughly 25,000, swelling past 100,000 with holiday visitors and second-home users in some periods; these are agency estimates for its service area, not a census of the entire valley.

## 2018–2019: Seasonal economy with infrastructure limits

Mountain roads connect the valley to the lower San Bernardino region and the High Desert through distinct routes. Heavy snow, ice, fire, rockfall or vehicle incidents can affect roads independently. Groceries, fuel, building materials and medical supplies depend substantially on deliveries. Short growing seasons and mountainous topography constrain local farming despite forests and the lake.

**Water baseline:** the City of Big Bear Lake Department of Water & Power says its supplies come entirely from local groundwater, wells and springs, with **no drinking water drawn from Big Bear Lake** and no imported supply from off mountain. Water storage, pumps, tanks, booster stations and distinct local providers matter. This is a system with renewable recharge limits, not a guarantee of infinite drinking water.

## 2020–2022: COVID, tourism, remote work and fire risk

The pandemic affects hospitality, visitor spending, schools and local healthcare while some essential services continue. Vacation and second homes, outdoor recreation, construction and remote work can influence housing and population demand, but the scale and direction need local annual data. A forest resort still has exposure through visitors, supplies and workers.

Southern California's broader wildfires show the importance of vegetation, wind, evacuation roads and utility systems; the specific 2020 state fire acreage should not be attributed to the Big Bear Valley unless a documented fire perimeter covers it. Drought and snowpack patterns affect groundwater recharge with lags, and electricity-dependent distribution requires local backups for continued operation during outages.

## February–March 2023: Extreme mountain snowstorm

San Bernardino County's emergency-management after-action report describes an exceptional storm beginning around **February 25, 2023**, following a National Weather Service blizzard warning on February 23. Heavy snow made roads impassable, complicated fuel delivery, affected electricity and communications and left some residents confined. The county declares an emergency February 27; the state and federal governments subsequently issue their own emergency declarations. County highways reopen in stages, with the report describing public reopening by March 12.

This is a particularly valuable **documented logistical stress test** for later worldbuilding. It reveals that even before a fictional energy catastrophe, snow-clearing equipment, fuel, crews, road access, communications and trained local responders can determine whether food and medicine reach mountain communities. The 2023 storm is an actual historical event; a later Plague/Cascade response must be separately modeled.

## 2024–November 2026: Ongoing hazards and regional connections

The region remains connected to the wider county economy, tourism, schools and highway network. Wildfire warnings and mountain evacuations require fire-specific and date-specific assessment. Rising insurance or construction costs, demand for housing, and changing local employment must not be assumed from statewide trends without local data. Big Bear's physical geography and groundwater dependence do not change because of advances in AI, though workplace automation elsewhere could later change residents' income or the cost of supplies.

**First fictional divergence:** December 2026, not before. In 2027–2029, the setting's rapid automation may change service work, scheduling, remote jobs and the tourism economy, but the extent has not been established.

## Infrastructure and research tasks for detailed worldbuilding

- Obtain water-system maps by provider, including wells, gravity-fed slant wells, booster stations, storage, pipeline sections and power backup. Treat the DWP service area separately from Big Bear City CSD and other providers.
- List roads by pass, grade, seasonal closure and year-round alternate access; include how snow plows are fueled and maintained.
- Compare permanent household counts, visitors and second-home occupancy; identify seasonal food demand and wastewater capacity.
- Identify clinics, hospitals, ambulance stations, pharmacies and medical travel routes.
- Inventory timber and agricultural capability realistically; forest land is not interchangeable with cropland.
- Document the February 2023 storm's distribution of closures and service outages at settlement level rather than attaching the same severity to every address.

**Historical conclusion:** the valley has physical water sources, skilled workers and a diverse population, but its survival capacity in a later catastrophe depends on functioning infrastructure, winter supply, medical access and food production. It is not already a post-collapse refuge in 2026.
