# The Pre-Divergence World (2019–17 November 2026)

**Historical scope:** actual world history from 1 January 2019 through **17 November 2026**, the last verified reference day. The New World's alternate history begins with the **fictional AGI announcement in December 2026**.

**World-bible rule:** this chapter is history *of Earth*, not documentation of the software used to build the setting. Entries record observed events and distinguish them from proposed causal interpretations. This is a deliberately selective synthesis, not an exhaustive chronology or a prediction. It establishes the political, economic, military, public-health, technological, environmental and demographic conditions that an alternate future inherits.

## Why the opening year is 2019

The future Great Plague will not be humanity's first epidemic in living memory. Its surviving adults will have memories of COVID-19, quarantine, vaccination, government emergency powers, interrupted schooling, altered workplaces, misinformation, medical capacity, and public debates over collective action. The first pandemic of the decade also provides a test case for supply networks, political institutions, and regional vulnerability.

However, the setting's **Great Plague beginning around 2030 remains a distinct, fictional disease crisis**. It is neither COVID-19 nor established to have the same pathogen, mortality profile, medical response or cause. Nothing in actual history proves that its fictional outcome must follow.

## 2019 — A connected, tense world

The world of 2019 is commercially interconnected and technologically sophisticated but politically unsettled. US–China trade tensions and tariffs affect manufacturing choices, agricultural exports, supply sourcing and confidence in the international trading order. Competition over semiconductors, telecommunications networks and intellectual property is already relevant to state security and industrial strategy.

In Hong Kong, major demonstrations begin in 2019 in opposition to an extradition proposal and broaden into demands concerning governance, police conduct and civil liberties. The protests and their later suppression become a major point of disagreement over Hong Kong's autonomy and China's national-security policy. Different governments describe these events differently; the world bible must report specific policies and documented actions rather than adopting a partisan summary as objective truth.

In December, clinicians in Wuhan, China, encounter clusters of pneumonia of unknown cause. On **31 December 2019**, the World Health Organization is notified. This is the start of the COVID-19 historical sequence.

**Historical importance:** a globally connected economy depends on ports, aviation, advanced manufacturing, concentrated industrial inputs and international trust. Its vulnerability does not require every institution to have been failing before the pandemic.

Sources: [WHO COVID timeline](https://www.who.int/europe/emergencies/situations/Covid-19); [Congressional-Executive Commission on China, Hong Kong 2019–2020](https://www.cecc.gov/publications/commission-analysis/rapid-deterioration-of-human-rights-in-hong-kong-after-passage-of).

## 2020 — The pandemic and emergency government

The novel coronavirus spreads internationally. The WHO declares a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on **30 January 2020** and characterizes COVID-19 as a pandemic on **11 March 2020**. Governments respond through varied combinations of restrictions, testing, public-health messaging, border controls, medical mobilization and fiscal interventions. Measures and outcomes vary widely by country and locality.

The immediate economic shock is severe: hospitality, aviation, commuting, retail and in-person services contract, while medical systems confront new demands. The IMF's April 2020 outlook projected a 3% contraction in global output, explicitly a **forecast at that date**, not a final estimate. Governments, central banks, employers, families and charities mobilize extraordinary support and adaptive strategies.

Schooling, office work and shopping move much more heavily online. Remote work becomes routine for some occupations but never becomes equally possible for trades, agricultural labor, delivery, care, emergency services or industrial work. Access to broadband, computers, savings and space at home influences how much disruption people suffer. Disrupted parts supply and concentrated chip production expose the fragility of just-in-time systems.

In the United States, the 2020 election takes place during the pandemic; Joe Biden defeats incumbent Donald Trump. Globally, experiences with emergency restrictions, public-health information and economic assistance differ; distrust does not follow one universal political pattern.

**Foundational questions for the setting:** which communities retained clinics, nurses, first responders, hospitals, community networks and remote-work infrastructure? Which families lost savings or access to education? Which institutions accumulated authority or mistrust?

Sources: [WHO chronology](https://www.who.int/emergencies/diseases/novel-coronavirus-2019/interactive-timeline); [IMF, The Great Lockdown, April 2020](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2020/04/14/weo-april-2020); [CDC pandemic timeline](https://www.cdc.gov/museum/timeline/covid19.html).

## 2021 — Uneven recovery and a changing security order

Vaccination programs broaden, mobility and economic activity resume at different speeds, and states begin confronting the consequences of deferred services, disrupted education, depleted inventories and unequal access to care. Pandemic-era fiscal support and recovering demand interact with shortages of shipping capacity, labor and components. Inflationary pressures begin to build in many economies. These are overlapping processes, not one exclusively political cause.

On **15 August 2021**, Taliban forces take Kabul amid the collapse of the Afghan republic and the withdrawal of US and allied forces. The change in government transforms women's and girls' rights, asylum and regional security, and prompts severe economic and humanitarian challenges. The event also changes perceptions of military commitments and state endurance.

Digital services, cloud platforms and online communication become more embedded in everyday commerce. The semiconductor shortage illustrates how a vehicle, communications device, industrial controller or medical machine may depend on components originating in a small number of globally distributed production systems.

**Foundational questions:** where did population movements or asylum communities develop? Which industrial facilities became more dependent on foreign inputs? How did post-pandemic public trust and fiscal capacity vary by locality?

Sources: [UN Afghanistan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022](https://afghanistan.un.org/sites/default/files/2022-01/afghanistan-humanitarian-needs-overview-2022_0.pdf); [World Bank, global growth and inflation analysis](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099031409142266105/pdf/IDU01ed08ada0dcb1048f809c390d3ba1acab4e2.pdf).

## 2022 — European war, price shocks and the public AI moment

On **24 February 2022**, Russia launches a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, expanding the war that began in 2014. The attack triggers extensive warfare, displacement, sanctions, diplomatic responses, and changes in European security planning. The United Nations General Assembly votes overwhelmingly in March to demand Russia's withdrawal. Ukraine's resistance and international assistance change the conflict's initial trajectory. The military, political and humanitarian consequences unfold over subsequent years; do not retroactively treat later outcomes as inevitable.

War, sanctions and disrupted agricultural and energy commerce intensify global pressures on fuel and food prices. Central banks in many economies raise interest rates to combat inflation, reshaping credit, mortgages, government borrowing and corporate investment. Simultaneously, industrial policy and trade controls make production of semiconductors and advanced hardware a geopolitical concern rather than only a question of lowest-cost global sourcing.

On **30 November 2022**, OpenAI publicly introduces ChatGPT, a GPT-3.5-family conversational assistant. GPT-3 itself was introduced in 2020. This event becomes one of The New World's defining technological milestones, **but not its first historical event**. Mass access to generative AI begins to change expectations concerning creative work, education, software, information, language and automation.

**Foundational questions:** how do energy prices, rearmament, consumer debt, skills shortages and technology concentration shape the communities that will later encounter AGI? Do military procurement and automated industrial systems leave behind repairable local assets or new dependencies?

Sources: [CFR conflict chronology](https://www.cfr.org/global-conflict-tracker/conflict/conflict-ukraine); [World Bank, inflation and Ukraine-war shock](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099031409142266105/pdf/IDU01ed08ada0dcb1048f809c390d3ba1acab4e2.pdf); [OpenAI, Introducing ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt/).

## 2023 — Widening wars and advanced generative AI

On **14 March 2023**, OpenAI announces GPT-4. More capable large language models and multimodal systems accelerate public and commercial experimentation with generative AI. Employers debate productivity, error rates, data protection, copyright, worker training and whether particular occupations should be reorganized. At this point, expanding technical capability is not evidence that the majority of workers have been replaced.

The WHO ends COVID-19's designation as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on **5 May 2023**. This does **not** mean that COVID-19 has ceased to exist. Health services and many households continue to deal with ongoing infections, consequences of illness and earlier disruptions.

Fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces begins on **15 April 2023**, causing extensive displacement, damage to civilian infrastructure and continuing humanitarian needs. On **4 April 2023**, Finland becomes NATO's 31st member, reflecting changes in European security expectations after Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

On **7 October 2023**, Hamas and other Palestinian armed groups attack Israeli communities and military sites, killing and abducting civilians as well as attacking military personnel. Israel launches large-scale military operations in Gaza. The resulting war causes extensive civilian casualties, destruction and displacement, and develops wider regional diplomatic and military consequences. Competing narratives about responsibility and war conduct must be attributed carefully, while factual civilian suffering and documented attacks are reported plainly.

From late 2023, attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden lead to route diversions around the Cape of Good Hope. These disruptions illustrate how a regional conflict can raise global costs and lengthen delivery times.

**Foundational questions:** what military stockpiles, humanitarian networks, refineries, shipping routes and migrant populations will exist when the fictional crises begin? Does increasingly software-mediated warfare create dual-use technological skills or more fragile infrastructure dependencies?

Sources: [OpenAI GPT-4](https://openai.com/index/gpt-4-research/); [WHO pandemic status](https://www.who.int/europe/emergencies/situations/Covid-19); [UN Sudan overview](https://www.un.org/en/spotlight-on-sudan/background); [NATO membership history](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/organization/nato-member-countries); [UN Israel/Gaza report](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4092971/files/A_80_502-EN.pdf); [UNCTAD maritime disruption](https://unctad.org/news/unprecedented-shipping-disruptions-raise-risk-global-trade-unctad-warns).

## 2024 — Political transitions, infrastructure exposure and AI governance

On **7 March 2024**, Sweden becomes NATO's 32nd member. Armed conflicts in Ukraine, Sudan and the Middle East continue to affect security expenditure, migration, humanitarian capacity and international relations. Red Sea shipping diversions and drought restrictions affecting the Panama Canal show that security and environmental constraints can interrupt major trade corridors simultaneously.

OpenAI introduces GPT-4o in **May 2024**, while other firms and open-model communities advance multimodal models and developer tools. Competition in training chips, cloud capacity, data centers, software platforms and electrical power increasingly ties AI development to physical infrastructure and national industrial strategies. The European Union's AI Act enters into force on **1 August 2024**, beginning a phased regulatory regime rather than applying every obligation immediately.

In the **5 November 2024** US presidential election, Donald Trump defeats Kamala Harris, winning 312 electoral votes to 226. This is a documented election result, not a judgment about the candidates or a prediction about later US politics. Other governments, including in Europe and Asia, confront differing public debates over inflation, migration, energy, security and economic competitiveness; avoid treating the electorate of any single country as representative of the world.

The WMO later confirms 2024 as the warmest year in its then-available global observational record, approximately **1.55°C above the 1850–1900 average**. A single calendar year above 1.5°C is not the same as the Paris Agreement's long-term warming threshold. Climate stress remains an independent variable in later food, water and settlement histories.

**Foundational questions:** energy grids and computing clusters, defense manufacturers, household affordability, coastal flood risk and water systems all deserve geographic mapping before speculative political borders are drawn.

Sources: [NATO Sweden accession](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2024/03/07/sweden-officially-joins-nato); [UNCTAD Red Sea trade](https://unctad.org/news/red-sea-crisis-and-implications-trade-facilitation-africa); [EU AI Act](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ai-act-enters-force-2024-08-01_en); [US National Archives election result](https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/2024); [WMO 2024 global climate](https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/wmo-confirms-2024-warmest-year-record-about-155degc-above-pre-industrial-level).

## 2025 — A new US administration, security spending and capital investment

Donald Trump is sworn in as the **47th US president on 20 January 2025**, beginning a second nonconsecutive term. Policy directions on trade, immigration, industrial production, foreign relations and energy become important to the starting conditions of the setting. Specific effects must be evaluated through dated and attributed economic evidence rather than political slogans, and developments after the November 2026 cutoff are not assumed.

Economic and technological competition increasingly overlap. Firms and states invest heavily in computing power, chip design, electricity and AI applications. Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports that global corporate AI investment more than doubled during 2025. That investment does not by itself establish profits, universal adoption, labor replacement or AGI.

Data-center expansion and electrical demand make dependable grids, substations, cooling water and backup power relevant to the next decade. A cloud-based automated economy has very different dependencies from a machine operating on a local controller with a technician able to repair it.

Armed conflicts and military planning remain a substantial claim on fiscal and industrial resources. SIPRI's April 2026 accounting estimates **$2.887 trillion** in global military expenditure during 2025 (a retrospective estimate, not a projection), the 11th consecutive year of increase in its series. This affects the inventory of armaments, aerospace and electronics facilities, trained personnel and military infrastructure potentially present before the Cascade.

**Foundational questions:** which governments prioritize AI capacity versus welfare and training? Where are data centers, electricity sources, defense works and specialized manufacturing located? What happens if a region loses personnel before it loses machines?

Sources: [US Senate inaugural record](https://www.inaugural.senate.gov/60th-inaugural-ceremonies/); [Stanford 2026 AI Index—Economy](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy); [SIPRI 2025 expenditure report, released April 2026](https://www.sipri.org/publications/2026/sipri-fact-sheets/trends-world-military-expenditure-2025).

## 2026 — The documented threshold, through 17 November

**This chapter stops at 17 November 2026.** Neither a full calendar-year summary nor a completed November–December outcome can be written honestly from that cutoff.

A changing trade regime, geopolitical rivalry, investment in AI infrastructure and pressure on international institutions remain significant features of the documented world. The WTO's 2026 world trade discussion describes serious ongoing disruptions to the multilateral trading system; its **March 2026 forecast** anticipates slower merchandise-trade growth, which should be represented as a forecast *at the time*, not a final full-year observation. Policies, implementation dates and trade effects differ by country.

The Sudan conflict continues to cause severe humanitarian problems. Warfare in Ukraine remains a major issue for European security. Developments in Gaza and the wider Middle East affect civilians, regional diplomacy, energy security and trade routes. Do not freeze any live conflict at its 2023 or 2024 position; use dated sources for country-specific baselines.

Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports extraordinary investment and growing organizational use of AI, but observed labor impacts are uneven. Research on young workers in AI-exposed occupations identifies emerging employment effects, yet **there is no basis to label the actual pre-divergence world already fully automated or overwhelmingly unemployed**. Those developments belong to The New World's speculative future.

The EU's AI Act continues phased implementation. Around the world, policy choices concerning AI safety, sovereignty, data access, labor protection and capital investment differ. The precise mix of state and corporate power on the eve of the fictional AGI announcement remains a fertile, unresolved historical question.

**17 November 2026 is the final documented historical reference date.** The next defining event—**a convincing public announcement of AGI in December 2026**—is an *established fictional divergence*, not an actual event in this documented history. The speculative years 2027–2029 are addressed separately in [The Brief Age of AI](20-brief-age-of-ai.md).

Sources: [WTO World Trade Report 2026](https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/publications_e/world-trade-report-2026_execsum_e.htm); [WTO March 2026 trade outlook](https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/booksp_e/gtos0326_e.pdf); [UN Sudan](https://www.un.org/en/spotlight-on-sudan/background); [Stanford AI Index 2026](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy); [EU AI Act timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/timeline/timeline-implementation-eu-ai-act).

## Structural forces inherited by the alternate world

### Political institutions and public legitimacy

There is no universally applicable story of governments becoming either stronger or weaker from 2019 to 2026. States acquire experience with emergency powers, public-health coordination, border controls, fiscal responses, digital services, protests, elections, and shifting alliances. In different places these leave enhanced capacity, contested legitimacy, fiscal liabilities, or increased community coordination.

**For worldbuilding:** research local governments, tribal institutions, churches, unions, NGOs, private employers, courts and public-health networks individually. A 2030 response will depend on **who the population already trusted**, who operated critical services and who could legally or practically enforce public-health measures.

### Political economy, ownership and labor

The pandemic causes a large recession and extraordinary policy responses; recovery and supply constraints contribute to inflation, changing interest rates and debt burdens. As AI investment rises, ownership of data, chips, energy and compute becomes increasingly important. The later fictional automation wave changes labor and wages on top of these preexisting conditions; it does not start from a neutral 2019 economy.

**For worldbuilding:** distinguish *employment* from *production*, *wealth ownership* from *regional self-sufficiency*, and *income loss* from *direct disaster mortality*. A region with large industrial output can have vulnerable residents if that output no longer supports local livelihoods.

### Military power, production and geography

European and Middle Eastern wars, Sudan's conflict, arms procurement, sanctions and NATO enlargement affect infrastructure, industrial capacity, stockpiles, logistics, displaced populations and alliance priorities. They do not mechanically predict the outcome of later fictional wars or successor governments.

**For worldbuilding:** trace barracks, repair depots, ports, warehouses, ammunition factories, industrial electricians and transport corridors. Military assets are not usable indefinitely without personnel, parts, supply and political command.

### Technology, energy and physical bottlenecks

The semiconductor supply chain, networking, electrical grids, cloud providers, water cooling, fuels and skilled maintenance are fundamental to both advanced AI and later repair capacity. Complex systems can fail from missing logistics without being physically destroyed. Similarly, local software and machinery can survive after network failure.

**For worldbuilding:** identify which automation systems were centralized, which had local controls, and which communities retained technicians capable of maintenance without original manufacturers.

### Demography, public health, migration and climate

The actual COVID-19 pandemic changes family experiences, migration, education and approaches to disease control. Different regions face distinct demographic and public-service conditions by the late 2020s. Climate change and extreme events affect water, heat, farming, coastal exposure and food supply independently of the later fictional catastrophes.

**For worldbuilding:** no unsourced universal mortality or migration coefficients. Use population baselines, age distribution, local water systems, food imports, housing and service capacity to build region-specific scenarios.

## Transition into speculative history

The New World's divergence starts **December 2026**. This world does **not** rewrite the origins of COVID-19, the 2022 invasion of Ukraine or the 2024 election unless a later explicit alternate-history decision says so.

From December 2026 to 2029, the brief Age of AI produces widespread—yet geographically and occupationally uneven—human labor displacement and accelerated automation. In or around 2030, the fictional Great Plague strikes societies already confronting economic and institutional strain. The later Cascade is **independent in cause**, damaging energy and communication systems; its Year 0 calendar anchor remains provisional.

**Causal model for future research:** inherited institutional capacity + geography + economic conditions + demographic structure + public health + military exposure + infrastructure dependence → differential response to AGI automation and labor displacement → differential plague resilience → migration and partial recovery → differential impact of the Cascade → divergent successor societies.

Every arrow is a **hypothesis to investigate locally**, not a universally proved deterministic law.
