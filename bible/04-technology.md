# Post-Cascade technology and material culture

**Rule:** physical artifacts and knowledge survive; industrial support systems do not necessarily survive.

## Viable technologies

| Technology | Plausible status | Principal constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Firearms | Work normally if mechanically intact and supplied | Ammunition, parts, maintenance and production; significant stockpiles could persist decades |
| Internal-combustion vehicles | Work if components and fuels are available | Initial damage scenario unresolved; fuel production/quality, tires, lubricants, parts |
| Batteries and electronics | Some survive | Grid disruption, component damage, replacement parts and capacity |
| Steam machinery | Viable for stationary and some transport uses | Boiler safety, machining, fuel, water, staffing |
| Waterwheels and windmills | Durable local power | Suitable sites, mechanical transmission, maintenance |
| Sailing and human-powered craft | Valuable on appropriate waters | Boats, charts, crews, tides, navigational hazards |
| Horses and other draft animals | Viable | Feed, breeding stock, veterinary care, harnesses, pastures |
| Bicycles and rail handcars | Viable with suitable routes | Bearings, tires, tracks, bridges and upkeep |

## Salvage culture

Dead vehicles become sources of wheels, springs, bearings, shafts, sheet steel and frames. Some can be adapted into **horse-drawn vehicles** where weight, braking and wheel/axle design permit. Roads can become wagon routes; hand-operated railcars may serve sections of maintained track. Bridges, ports, tanks, warehouses, mills, books and workshops are strategic legacies.

Do not assume that a full car body makes an efficient carriage: weight, suspension, animal traction, brakes and tire longevity constrain conversions. More durable purpose-built wagons may eventually replace novelty conversions.

## Steam power

Steam should have an important *limited* role: stationary mills and workshops are easier to sustain than locomotives. Rail engines and steamboats require dependable water, fuel, heavy repair facilities, trained crews and routes whose demand justifies their operation. It is an industrial resource, not unlimited free energy.

## Technological diversity

At Year 50, some districts may use almost entirely animal and water power; others may maintain gunsmithing and small rail/steam systems; rare enclaves could rebuild partial electrical systems. A universal medieval reset would be inconsistent with surviving knowledge, equipment and varied geography.
